# AmbuMed service gap analysis and implementation sequence

Status date: 7 September 2026  
Owners: product, clinical safety, engineering, implementation, AmbuMed clinical/operations/finance approvers

## Executive decision

AmbuMed's [first-party services page](https://www.ambumed.org/services-2) verifies 13 advertised service headings and confirms 80 beds across semi-private, private and VIP admission rooms. It does not publish tariffs, deposit rules, department schedules, detailed forms or permission policy. TamamHealth must configure those from approved hospital material, never infer them from marketing copy.

A follow-up image supplied for this review names Pharmacy, Paediatrics, Physiotherapy, Obstetrics & Gynaecology, Advanced Operation Theatre, and X-ray and fluoroscopy services. Pharmacy and radiography overlap the original list; the combined scope therefore contains **17 distinct service domains**. The image is treated as requested scope, not independent evidence of operational readiness.

This release begins the shared foundation across the platform and website. It ships first-class Department records, appointment linkage with backwards compatibility, queue/report summary logic, a controlled specialty vocabulary, configurable room classes/tariff/deposit fields, new billing catalog categories, and honest public delivery-state copy. It does **not** claim that the specialist modules below are complete.

## Shipped in this tranche

| Capability | Implementation | Safety / compatibility rule |

| Department entity and editor | `DepartmentDoc` in the already-synced, org-scoped hospitals database; deterministic facility-local IDs; active state; queue flag; specialty links; clinic-day schedule slots; facility settings UI for queue and clinic hours | Existing settings strings remain readable; saving departments promotes them to documents; removed departments deactivate rather than delete; published hours are explicitly not treated as live room/provider availability |
| Department queue/report foundation | Appointment-derived queue sorted by priority/time; summary counts and mean wait; booking edits resolve or clear the stable department ID; appointments can be filtered into department worklists | Facility/org `DataScope` is mandatory; legacy string matching applies only when no ID exists |
| Specialty vocabulary | Stable codes for the AmbuMed-relevant physician specialties, including paediatrics, obstetrics/gynaecology and physiotherapy, with explicit legacy aliases | Unknown free text is not guessed; existing user documents remain readable; no new professional role is implied |
| Room classes/commercial terms | General, semi-private, private and VIP definitions; optional nightly tariff and deposit; class/terms snapshot fields on admission | No price is seeded; blank means not approved/configured |
| Billing catalog | Dental, dialysis, optical, cardiac diagnostics, physiotherapy/rehabilitation and admission deposit categories plus optional billing unit | Categories are not prices; facilities must approve every catalogue row |
| Public website | “Available now” and “In delivery” status section; PACS and patient-portal claims corrected | Planned capabilities are labeled; facility database isolation is not described as complete |

## Shared architecture and safety requirements

- Every operational and PHI document must carry `orgId` and facility linkage and every read must accept `DataScope`. Missing tenant context fails closed.
- New synced document types must be registered in the database type allow-list and write-permission matrix together. Department writes are restricted to existing facility-configuration roles.
- Local PouchDB remains the workflow write path; services emit sync events and audit events. UI writes must not be rerouted through APIs merely to appear centralized.
- Department IDs are additive. Historical appointments with strings continue to work and are matched only as a fallback.
- Specialty codes are TamamHealth workflow identifiers, not diagnoses. Diagnoses continue to use controlled ICD-11 identifiers; WHO recommends the MMS linearization for ICD-11 coding via its [API](https://icd.who.int/docs/icd-api/APIDoc-Version2/).
- Mental-health and clinical-photo records must not ship until authorization, consent, access review, offline storage, export and audit behavior are designed together.
- `docs/FACILITY-ISOLATION.md` remains controlling: selector-based replication plus client filtering reduces exposure but is not complete database-level authorization. None of this tranche changes or closes that limitation.

## Sequenced backlog and acceptance gates

1. **Foundation completion:** migrate remaining provider/referral surfaces to department IDs; approved room inventory; deposit invoice/refund flow; catalogue bulk import. Gate: cross-facility negative tests, conflict tests, offline round trip, audit review.
2. **High-volume specialty units:** dialysis, dental, theatre, paediatrics, physiotherapy and obstetrics/gynaecology. Gate each on signed SOP/form/catalogue, clinician UAT, role matrix, downtime workflow and safety-case review.
3. **Exam/diagnostic specialties:** ophthalmology/optical, cardiac diagnostics, mhGAP/restricted mental health. Gate on device/interface evidence and privacy policy.
4. **Polish:** dermatology photography/procedures, radiographer acquisition role, patient booking by active Department, validated department/HMIS reports.

## Validation pack required from AmbuMed

- Signed department/service directory with locations, hours, clinic days and accountable lead.
- De-identified versions of each current paper form and register.
- Approved room/bed inventory, tariff sheet, deposit/refund policy and currency rules.
- Role-to-task matrix, especially acquisition vs interpretation, prescribing, dispensing, theatre and mental-health access.
- Device inventory and supported exports/interfaces for CT, ECG, echo, Holter and dialysis machines.
- Clinical photography, consent, retention, disclosure and emergency-access policies.
- Report list required by hospital management, Ministry of Health, payers and auditors.

## Evidence notes

The implementation recommendations use [HL7 FHIR HealthcareService](https://hl7.org/fhir/healthcareservice.html) for service/schedule relationships, [FHIR ChargeItem](https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/chargeitem-definitions.html) for charge attribution, [FHIR DiagnosticReport](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/diagnosticreport.html) for diagnostic workflows, the [WHO Surgical Safety Checklist manual](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598590), [WHO mhGAP-IG 2.0](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549790), [CDC dialysis safety tools](https://www.cdc.gov/dialysis-safety/hcp/tools/index.html), and [ISO 3950:2016](https://www.iso.org/standard/68292.html). These are design inputs, not claims that AmbuMed currently uses a particular standard.
